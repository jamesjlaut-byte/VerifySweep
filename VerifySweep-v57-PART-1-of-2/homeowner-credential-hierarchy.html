HOMEOWNER GUIDE

Chimney certifications explained: what should you actually look for?

A credential is not a guarantee that every job will be done correctly. But checking independently verifiable credentials gives you information that a Google ranking, advertisement or review count cannot provide: evidence that a named person has completed a recognized education or testing process.

1. Core chimney certification

For chimney sweeping, inspection and venting work, look for an individual credential that directly covers chimney and venting knowledge. CSIA’s Certified Chimney Sweep is one widely recognized example. NCSG also maintains Certified Chimney Professional programs.

2. Hearth and fuel-specific specialization

NFI certifications focus on planning and installation knowledge for hearth systems, with specialties including woodburning, gas, pellet and hearth design. These can be especially relevant when the work involves fireplaces, stoves, inserts or their venting systems.

3. Advanced inspection and specialized education

Some professionals pursue additional inspection-oriented credentials, advanced chimney education, manufacturer-specific training, code classes and continuing education. These can broaden a technician’s knowledge beyond a foundational credential.

4. Membership is not the same as certification

Membership in a professional association can show industry involvement and access to education, but membership should not automatically be described as an individual technical certification. VerifySweep keeps these categories separate.

The most important question

Who is actually coming to my home, what credential does that person hold, and can I verify that credential with the issuing organization?

A company may employ a certified professional without every employee holding that same credential. Ask who will perform or supervise your work.
